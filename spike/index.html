Phase 0 spike

A hardcoded drum loop and bass line through a lookahead scheduler, sent to a SoundFont synth and to Web MIDI. Plus a keyboard rollover and input latency test.

Chrome, Edge or Firefox. Needs HTTPS or localhost, and a permission prompt.

2 · Loop: tempo hold test

Positive plays later. Enable both outputs and adjust until they line up.

Pass: runs 5+ minutes with late events = 0. The scheduler timer runs in a worker, so a background tab should not break it; try switching tabs. Kit on channel 10, bass on channel 2, live keys on channel 3.

3 · Keyboard rollover

Hold all three keys of each zone at once. A zone turns green when the keyboard reports all three together. Zones that never turn green are blocked by this keyboard's matrix.

0 of 12 zones passed Most keys held at once: 0

Keys also play: bottom row hits drum pads, home row plays bass, top two rows play lead. Try chords across zones while a drum key is held.

4 · Input latency

Tap test: with the loop playing, tap Space on every beat for a few bars. The offset is how far your taps land from the beat as heard (negative is early). A steady offset near zero means key-to-ear latency is fine; a large positive mean is latency you would feel when recording. Bluetooth headphones add 100+ ms, so test on wired output or speakers.

Done when

  • The loop holds tempo for 5+ minutes with no late events.
  • The same stream plays on a MIDI port in Chrome, in time with the SoundFont output.
  • You know whether three-key zones work on your laptop keyboard, and whether latency is acceptable.
